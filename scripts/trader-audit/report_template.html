Who's actually worth following

FOMO thesis vs Pump callout

The fair version of the platform question: each trader's first thesis on a token (FOMO) against each trader's first callout on a token (pump.fun). Both are "here's my call" posts. Same entry for both, the first 1-minute candle after the post, scored at two horizons after 3% costs: 1 hour (take profit at +50%, stop at -30%, out at 1h) and 24 hours (take profit at 3x, stop at -40%, out at 24h). Market cap at a thesis is derived from the token's supply (seen on stored trades) times the price at the post.

Which alerts are worth sending: thesis vs callout

Every first thesis (FOMO) and first callout (pump.fun) per trader per token in the window, scored the same way: a follower acting ~1 minute after the post, after 3% costs. "All three exits" is the average of a quick flip (+50% / -30%, out by 1h), a 2x flip (2x / -30%, out by 4h) and a runner (3x / -40%, out by 24h).

Action plan, in order of impact

    Alert levels

    Instead of choosing traders, a user picks how many alerts an hour they want and the bot keeps their list filled with the best-rated traders that fit, updating as ratings refresh. Levels nest: moving up adds traders, never drops one. Shown for a user receiving every alert type; the app recomputes per user from their own settings.

    Cut list

    Judged by what a follower got entering on the alert, not by the trader's own PnL: a trader who dumps on followers but whose calls still run stays in. Hard = strong evidence, remove from tracking. Soft = weak, keep tracked but leave out of presets and defaults.

    Lowest market-cap callers

    Median market cap at entry (FOMO first buys) or at callout (Pump), for traders with 20+ calls in the window. Tiny caps plus high volume usually means noise, but not always. The follower columns show which ones actually pay.

    Does this hold up?

    The test that matters: rate traders on one half of the window, then check what followers got in the other half. Done both ways.
